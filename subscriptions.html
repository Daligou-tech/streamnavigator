How it works

You see what we found before you decide whether to buy the report.

  1. List them, or drop in a statement

    A bank or card statement fills in the names and the prices for you. Typing them yourself works just as well.

  2. Three taps per subscription

    When you last used it, whether you would miss it, and who else uses it. About fifteen seconds each — and those three answers are what decide the recommendation.

  3. Get your free scorecardfree

    How many lines are worth acting on and what they are worth a year, worked out in your browser before anything is sent anywhere.

  4. Decide before buying the report

    If the scorecard finds nothing, you will know that for free. One flat price either way.

  5. Receive your report in your inbox

    A call on every line, ordered by what is at stake: what you pay, what you told us, the figure, and what to do — with dates on the ones that need one.

Five calls, not three

The difference between two of these is where the money actually is. Most subscriptions cannot be paused: on those, “rotate” means cancelling and signing up again later at whatever the price is then. We hold a pause policy per service and we will not use the word when it is not true.

KeepYou are getting what you pay for.
DowngradeYou want it. You do not want this tier.
RotateA genuine hold exists. Off now, back on a named date.
CancelStop paying. Coming back means signing up again.
ReviewThe decision is not ours to make. We say why.

Three things we will not tell you to cancel

  • A year you have already paid for. An annual plan used twice looks like the worst line on the page. Cancelling it part-way through refunds nothing and in some categories costs a termination fee on top. We give you the date the decision is actually due, and what it is worth.
  • Something somebody else in the house uses. One person cannot cancel a family plan on their own say-so, and a report that tells them to is worth less than nothing.
  • Something holding your own files. “Barely used” is the commonest answer about cloud storage and the worst possible reason to cancel it. Above the free tier, the provider starts deleting what does not fit.

    What this does not do

    Read this before you start.

    • We do not connect to your bank. There is no login, no account access and no read-only link. You tell us what you pay for, or you show us a statement.
    • We do not cancel anything for you. You get the call, the figure and the step. You make every change yourself.
    • We do not know what anything will cost if you come back. Prices rise and promotional rates do not return. Where that matters we say so instead of pretending otherwise.
    • We do not hold a checked cancellation link for every service. We hold one for twenty of them. For the rest we tell you where the setting lives in your own account, and we do not invent a URL.
    • We do not put a number on a line you did not price. No price, no figure. The report says which lines those are and asks you for the amount.
    • Some decisions are not ours. Shared plans, bundles and anything holding your files come back as a review with the reason named. That is the product working, not a gap in it.
    • We cannot make the work go away. Acting on a report that cancels six things takes about an hour, and two of them will want a phone call. The report puts them in order and starts you on the largest, but you do the hour.

    Questions

    Do I need to connect my bank account?
    No, and there is no option to. You list your subscriptions, or upload a statement as a PDF or a photo. No bank login is ever required, and the free scorecard is computed in your browser without sending anything at all.
    Why not just read my own bank statement?
    A statement tells you what you are charged. It does not tell you which of fourteen charges is safe to stop, which service cannot be paused, which one loses a price you will never get back, or which date to turn something on again — and it will not show you an annual charge that fired eight months ago. The decision is the work, and the statement does not do it.
    What is the difference between rotate and cancel?
    A rotate is a genuine hold: the account stays as it is and you resume on a date. A cancel is a cancel — you re-sign later at whatever the price is then. Most services do not offer a hold, so most of the time “rotate” would be a lie. We hold a pause policy per service and use the right word.
    How is this different from the streaming tool on the homepage?
    The streaming tool is purpose-built for video: it checks when the shows and sports you actually follow are on, and dates your recommendations against them. This covers everything else you pay for — software, memberships, boxes, storage, apps — and works from what you tell us rather than a TV schedule. If streaming is your whole problem, use that one; it is $19.99 a year and it is better at it.
    Will you cancel things for me?
    No. You get the call, the amount, the date and the step. You make every change yourself.
    What if you find nothing?
    You will know that before you pay — the scorecard shows it free. What the report gives you then is every line named, with what you told us about it and why keeping it is right. If the report misses what it promised, we refund in full.
    Why do you need three answers per subscription?
    Because they are what decide the recommendation, and nothing else can supply them. A statement knows the money; only you know whether you would miss it. Without those answers we could tell you what to think about, but not what to do — and that is not worth paying for.

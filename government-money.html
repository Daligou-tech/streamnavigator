How it works

Narrowing, not guessing. You still confirm every figure — we tell you exactly where.

  1. Tell us about your household

    Where you live, whether you own or rent, how many people, roughly what the household earns, whether you expect to owe federal income tax, and anything you have bought, installed or changed in the last couple of years. Two minutes of typing.

  2. Pay $19

    One flat price, one report. Nothing renews and there is no account.

  3. Your situation is matched against how these programs work

    Federal, state, utility and local categories, checked against what you told us. We do not hold a live program database and we do not look up today’s dollar amounts — see what this does not do, below.

  4. You get a shortlist with a source on every line

    What each program is, why your answers point at it, what would rule you out, the ones we ruled out already and why, and the office that can confirm the current amount and deadline.

Four things this report will not count

These are the reasons to trust the rest of it. Every one is a way a confident-sounding report makes a household worse off.

A credit against tax you do not oweMost of the big residential credits reduce what you owe rather than paying you. In a year you owe nothing they are worth nothing, and a report that hands you the headline figure anyway has told you something false about your own money. Where you have not told us, every affected line says so.
A cap, quoted as if it were a paymentHeadline figures are almost always a ceiling worked out from what you actually spent. We say what governs the amount and send you to the authority that publishes it, rather than putting a number on it.
Two programs on one purchase, added togetherA utility or state rebate often reduces the amount a federal credit is calculated on, so they do not simply stack. Where two programs touch the same purchase we say so and give you the order to work them out in.
A discovery you already madeWhoever sold you the solar, the EV or the heat pump almost certainly mentioned the credit at the time. Those come back as “confirm you have already claimed this”, because telling you what you already know is not worth $19.

What this does not do

Read this before you start. It is most of what separates this from a confident-sounding guess.

  • We do not hold a live program database and we do not look programs up. This runs on general knowledge of how these programs work, which has a cutoff and is not guaranteed to match today’s rules. That is exactly why every line comes with the office or document that can confirm the current amount, eligibility and deadline.
  • We do not tell you what you will get. Most of these figures are ceilings worked out from what you actually spent, several reduce tax owed rather than paying out, and rebates often change what a credit is calculated on. We tell you which programs apply and what governs the amount. You confirm the number.
  • We do not ask for a login, a bank connection, a tax return or a Social Security number. There is no option to give us any of them. Everything here works from what you type into the form.
  • We do not file anything for you and we never contact an agency or a utility on your behalf. You make every claim yourself, or your tax preparer does.
  • We cannot know whether a program still has funding. Rebate pots close when they empty and reopen on a new fiscal year, and nothing here can see that. Where it matters we say to ask before you spend.
  • Without a state, the report is federal-only. We will not guess where you live, so the form will not let you buy until you have said. That is the one thing we insist on.
  • We cannot make the work go away. A shortlist of six programs is an evening of phone calls and forms. The report puts them in order and tells you who to call first, but you make the calls.

Questions

Why can’t I just search for this myself?
You can, and for one specific program you probably should. What takes the time is the other direction — working out which of hundreds of programs are even worth reading about, given that you rent, or that you will owe no federal tax this year, or that you have already used this year’s cap. That narrowing is what you are buying. The confirming you still do yourself, and every line tells you where.
Will you tell me exactly how much I’ll get?
No, and anyone who does is guessing. Most of these figures are ceilings computed from what you actually spent; several are credits against tax owed, which are worth nothing to a household that owes nothing; and a utility rebate often reduces the amount a federal credit is calculated on. We tell you which programs apply, what governs the amount, and where to compute it.
How current is this?
Not live, and we will not pretend otherwise. This runs on general knowledge of how these programs work — which has a training cutoff and is not guaranteed to match today’s rules. That is exactly why every program on your shortlist comes with the office or document that can confirm the current amount, eligibility and deadline. Treat our descriptions as “worth checking”, never as “what you will get”.
Do you file the claims for me?
No — you get clear, step-by-step next steps for each program, but you, or your tax preparer, submit the actual claim. We never contact an agency or a utility on your behalf.
Why do you insist on knowing my state?
Because federal programs are the same everywhere and everything else is not. State credits, county exemptions and utility rebates are scoped to a place, and they are most of what is worth finding. Without one, the report could only cover the federal side and then tell you so — which is a thin thing to have paid for. A ZIP code works too, and tells us which utility territory you are in.
What if I don’t qualify for anything?
The report says so in the first line, and lists what was checked and the specific fact that ruled each one out. If there is nothing on your report worth acting on, reply to the delivery email and we refund it in full.
Does this cover anything besides energy?
Yes — property tax relief, first-home programs, childcare, education, and assistance programs gated on income and household size all come up, depending on what you tell us. Energy is simply where the most money has been recently, which is why it comes up most.

How it works

The scorecard comes first. You see the number of findings, and which parts of your document we could not check, before you decide whether the report is worth buying.

  1. Upload your Closing Disclosure

    That is the only document we need. If your Loan Estimates or purchase contract fit in the same upload, add them — they unlock further checks and cost you nothing extra.

  2. Answer two quick questions

    Your property type, and whether your lender gave you a written list of service providers. About fifteen seconds. The second one decides which tolerance rule your fees fall under, so it changes what we can conclude.

  3. Get your free scorecardFree

    Within a few minutes you get the count of findings, the total dollars they touch, and a plain list of anything we could not read or verify on your document. No card, no charge, no obligation.

  4. Decide, then pay $59

    What this does not do

    Worth reading before you upload, so nothing in the report surprises you.

    • We do not tell you a fee is too expensive. We check whether figures are correct and whether rules were followed. Whether a title company charges more than the one down the road is not something we can verify, so we do not claim to.
    • We do not shop for you, negotiate for you, or contact your lender. You send the emails.
    • We read what you send us. If a scan is unclear, we say which lines we could not read rather than guessing at them — that list is in your free scorecard, before you pay.
    • Most closing costs are settled by the time your disclosure arrives. We mark clearly which findings are still worth a phone call and which are not.
    • This is automated analysis, not legal or mortgage advice. Every finding shows the arithmetic or the rule behind it so you can check it yourself, and take it to your lender, attorney or settlement agent.

    Questions

    What documents do I need?
    Only your final Closing Disclosure. Your Loan Estimates unlock the tolerance checks, and your purchase contract lets us check that the seller credits you negotiated actually landed. Both are useful, neither is required, and neither costs extra.
    How is this different from reading the document myself?
    Reading it tells you what you are being charged. It does not tell you whether the number is right. We re-run the arithmetic, test the prepaid interest and escrow cushion against the rules that govern them, look for charges billed twice, and rank everything by how many dollars it moves.
    Do you compare my fees to what other people paid?
    No. We used to describe something like that and we removed it, because we could not stand behind it. Telling you a fee is high without solid ground is how you end up challenging a legitimate charge the day before closing. Everything in your report is either arithmetic from your own document or a federal rule we can name.
    Will you contact my lender for me?
    No. You get drafted emails for your lender and your settlement agent, each naming the charge, the amount, the arithmetic or rule behind it, and what you are asking for. You send them yourself. We are not a law firm and this is not legal advice.
    What if my document is a bad scan?
    We tell you. Your free scorecard names every line we could not read clearly, so you know what is and is not covered before you spend anything. If too much is unreadable we will say so and suggest you send a cleaner copy rather than take your money.
    What if you find nothing?
    The free scorecard shows the count before you pay, so you are never buying a report to discover it is empty. If you do buy and the report does not contain the findings your scorecard promised, reply and we refund you in full.
    How long does it take?
    The scorecard is usually ready within five minutes, and the full report within five minutes of payment. You have three business days between receiving your Closing Disclosure and signing — this is built to fit inside that window.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Your next audit tells you, from your own paperwork rather than by asking. It reads the findings from your last report against the documents you send this time and reports each one as fixed, part-way, still there, or not testable — and totals what is confirmed recovered. That total only counts money a document proves: mortgage insurance that has disappeared from a statement, rent that actually went up. A statement that adds up this year does not prove last year’s difference was refunded, so we report it as resolved without putting a number on it. And if you send fewer documents than last time, the findings they would have answered are marked not testable, never fixed.
One charge of $149 covers up to four audits of the same property over twelve months — the first one now, three more whenever your figures change. There is no recurring charge and nothing to cancel; when the twelve months are up it simply stops. We also email you before each lease on the property ends, with what your own report found about that unit, because a renewal is the point most of these findings can actually be acted on. Every one of those emails carries an unsubscribe link.
Yes, and it is the one comparison that does not rely on what is typical anywhere else — it measures your building against itself twelve months earlier. Send two statements, or one that prints a prior-year column, and the report shows which cost lines rose faster than the rest. Your next audit within the year is compared against the one we already hold, so you do not have to send the old figures again. If the two periods cover different lengths of time or overlap, we say so and decline the comparison rather than pro-rating figures to make them line up.

If you have three or more units of the same floorplan, yes — we compare each one against the median of the others in your own building, which is a comparison we can prove from your rent roll.

What actually happened

Eighteen findings, eight commits, one day. The pattern worth carrying forward is not that the list got done — it is which things turned out to be wrong, and how they were found. Every one of the five extra defects was invisible to the layer it was written at, and visible the moment something rendered it the way a customer would see it.

The single most dangerous finding, C-1, was one line: the object handed to the tolerance engine did not carry the Closing Disclosure section, so charges that carry no tolerance under federal law were being tested at zero tolerance. That produced demands for refunds the customer was not owed, in a letter to the institution funding their mortgage. assignBucket() had been correct the whole time.

The same shape recurred four more times. A check correct in isolation, wrong where it was consumed. It is worth naming as a class, because three of the five were found only after deciding to look at rendered output rather than at return values.

The one thing that could not be done

No live paid run happened. The Anthropic account ran out of credits mid-test and was deliberately not topped up. So document extraction, and how well the model populates the new risk_signals and restrictions fields, are Unable to verify and remain so. Everything downstream of the model is verified against documents whose answers are known in advance. That is most of the value and not all of it, and the gap is specific rather than general.

The failure did verify one thing by accident: the customer-facing behaviour when the model is unavailable. The free scorecard returned "this is on our side, not your document", charged nothing, and left the row clean.

C-1 Fixed e9996a9

Tolerance bucketing lost the document section, turning untolerated charges into demands for money back

What shipped

One line — section: li.section in the cdCharges object — plus a comment explaining why it matters. A Section H owner's title policy goes back to no_tolerance, where 1026.19(e)(3)(iii) puts it; a Section C charge with a written provider list goes back to the 10% aggregate.

It also un-broke check 23, which could not fire at all because nothing ever landed in the basket to overflow it. The planted-defect suite went from 11 of 12 to 12 of 12.

How it is held

tests/closing-tolerance-buckets.test.js asserts the bucket for one charge per section, and three end-to-end tests through runDocumentAudit — because the rule was always right and only the plumbing broke. One asserts a $600 increase in an optional owner's policy produces no accusation; a companion asserts a real $300 Section A increase still does, so the guard cannot be bought by suppressing findings.

H-1 Fixed e9996a9

The HOA Risk Score is now computed, not chosen

What shipped

scoreRisk() derives the level from the signals the rubric already named, asked for as data in a new required risk_signals block rather than as a judgment. Every threshold is one the rubric already stated in prose — this makes the existing opinion executable, it does not invent a new one.

The model still commits to a read, which measurably improves the rationale, but the read is overwritten and a divergence is logged. risk_score_basis names every rule that fired and renders on the report, so a buyer can check the score rather than trust it.

The decision worth keeping

A package establishing nothing falls back to the model's read and says so. A Low earned by an absence of documents would be the worst output this product could produce, and a property test asserts it cannot happen.

C-3Withdrawn6568f32 · 3f7e1d9

Statutory benchmarking shipped, then was removed on the owner's decision — and the decision was right

What happened

This audit recommended building a statutory rate corpus. It was built: Virginia on 13 Sep, the District of Columbia the same day, every component citing its statute, with a completeness gate so an entry that could not prove it had enumerated every component was allowed to reconcile but never to accuse. It worked end to end.

The owner's instruction on 14 Sep was to remove all benchmarking. It is gone — machinery included, not merely switched off: the corpus file, compareToBenchmark(), the supplier and its injection point, the suppression list, two severities nothing can emit, three scorecard fields, and the coverage panel.

Why the reversal is the better call

The argument in this document was that a transfer tax is published law rather than a market opinion, and on a $375,000 purchase it is the largest figure on the settlement statement with a provably correct value. That is true and it is not the deciding fact.

A rate corpus is a maintenance obligation, and its failure mode is a false accusation. Jurisdictions move at their own pace, nothing in the product notices when one does, and a rate that has gone quietly stale produces a confident finding with a dollar figure attached — aimed at the settlement agent the customer still has to close with. Deleting a corpus costs an afternoon. Discovering through a customer that it is a year out of date costs the thing the product sells.

The audit weighed the capability and underweighted the obligation. Worth recording as a correction to the audit, not just to the code.

What the product says now

Every finding rests on the customer's own documents or on a federal rule, and nothing on an outside figure. That is a narrower claim than the one this document recommended, and it is one the product can keep without anybody maintaining anything. The catalog went back to 28 checks. Check 29, the note rate against the Loan Estimate, stayed — it compares two of the customer's own documents, which is what the TRID checks do and the opposite of consulting a corpus.

What the audit missed

Five defects surfaced while implementing the fixes. None was in the audit. Four were in code the audit had examined and not faulted, and one was introduced by the fixes themselves. They share a shape worth naming.

Each was correct at the layer it was written at and wrong at the layer the customer meets. Every unit test passed in all five cases. Three were found only after deciding to stop asserting on return values and start rendering output the way a customer would receive it — building the letters, running the harness, walking the pipeline end to end.

M-1Was livefe2c816

Check 29 priced a percentage in the letter the customer signs

What it produced
3. Your interest rate is higher than the Loan Estimate
   Charged $6.88; the figure I get is $6.50 — a difference of $0.38.

charged, expected and variance are money fields to every consumer of a finding — the letters render them through money(), the scorecard totals them, the PDF prints them — and the rate went into them.

Why it mattered

A buyer asking their lender to explain a $0.38 discrepancy on their mortgage rate does not get a second letter. The two genuine findings above it in the same message — $300 on a zero-tolerance charge, $339.04 of over-billed prepaid interest — go down with it. Shipped the previous day; every unit test on the check passed, because they asserted on the finding object and nothing rendered it.

Fixed

The rate lives in detail; the basis says it in words. Titles also lost "your", for the reason reconcileContract already documented: a title is printed in the report and used as the letter's numbered heading, where "your interest rate" flips to meaning the lender's. Two new guards are written against the class rather than the instance — no letter item may quote a figure under a dollar, and no heading may say "your".

M-2Was live1253413

The offline harness was auditing a different engine than customers get

Detail

scripts/audit-harness.js called runClosingAudit — the raw engine — while the free scorecard and the paid report both call runDocumentAudit. That is precisely the defect closing-report-parity.test.js exists for, written after the paid report was caught doing the same thing. The tool whose stated job is catching features wired to nothing had the same bug.

It showed six "cannot benchmark" rows no customer sees, ran none of the loan maths — eight of the checks the page names — and never exercised the transfer-tax check. Every clean result it had ever reported was clean on a smaller audit than the one that ships.

M-3Was live1253413

Section E put four spurious findings on an ordinary document

Detail

A Loan Estimate carries one "Recording Fees and Other Taxes" line; a Closing Disclosure breaks it into four. Line-by-line matching never succeeds, so every customer who supplied an LE got four "does not appear on the Loan Estimate" rows. No dollar impact, so nothing was claimed — but a customer gets one letter's worth of their lender's attention, and four rows of noise is how a real finding stops being read. Recording fees are already tested in the 10% aggregate and the taxes against statute, so nothing was lost by skipping them.

The upgrade panel advertised "Escrow cushion — against the RESPA legal maximum" among the checks that had already run on the uploaded document. The engine had always declined to test the cushion from a Closing Disclosure, correctly. claims.test.js caught it the instant the catalog changed — a test the audit had credited and which turned out to be protecting a claim that was already false.

M-6Was live3f7e1d9

Both products claimed knowledge of state law that neither engine has

Detail

This audit checked both products for overclaiming and did not catch any of it.

Closing's meta description promised "the fees your state and county set by law", and its lede offered "Any state." — coverage framed as something the product has, when what it has is checks that do not depend on where you are. The scorecard echoed the county and state back, which reads as a locality-aware analysis.

HOA's was worse, because it was an instruction rather than a sentence. The rubric told the model to raise "Florida structural integrity reserve study … post-Surfside, California balcony/exterior element inspections", guarded by "only raise these when the documents indicate the property is actually in that jurisdiction". That guard asks the model to infer a location and then apply remembered law to it — the "fact asserted from memory" class this document names as contradiction class 4, with a statute in place of a specification. The free check's page also stated three jurisdictions' review periods and package entitlements as fact.

None of it is left. HOA now reports a legal obligation only where the documents themselves describe one, and what a buyer is owed points at their agent or attorney.

M-7Latentfdc837d

A retired feature left behind a script armed to restore it

Detail

Removing benchmarking cancelled the next deployment. Vercel reported CANCELED and the log said BUILD BLOCKED, which reads like a failing test suite and was not — all 70 suites passed. scripts/fix-overpromises.js exited 1 in the patch-script step, and ci.sh is the ignored-build-step gate, so its exit code cancels the deploy.

That script existed to make benchmarking copy honest: six edits narrowing claims, and a seventh inserting the pre-payment coverage disclosure. It decided whether it had already run by looking for benchmark_coverage in the scorecard renderer. Deleting that panel removed its marker, so it concluded it had never run and tried to apply itself.

Had it succeeded it would have put benchmarking back into the page. That is the part worth carrying forward: a migration script that outlives the feature it migrates is not idle, it is armed. ci.sh already carried a warning about this exact trap, written when the rate-corpus freshness step was removed with the corpus — same shape, one step along, and it caught nobody.

How it was verified without a live run

Credits ran out, so the usual end-to-end test was closed off. What replaced it turned out to find more than the live run would have — because a live run tells you what happened, and a document with known answers tells you whether it was right.

Where this leaves both products

Are they ready for paid traffic?

Closing: yes. The critical defect is fixed, the letters are verified figure by figure, and the page describes what the engine does.
HOA: yes, with the first reports read closely. Everything that blocked it is shipped. What has not been established is whether the model populates risk_signals and restrictions well from real documents — the post-processing is proven, its inputs are not.

What should be watched first?

The first live HOA report: whether risk_signals comes back populated rather than all -1, whether restrictions finds the leasing cap, and whether the now-five sibling object fields hold without malformed sections — six was the shape that correlated with the /buying tag leak. Then the first closing report with a Loan Estimate, to see the rate check and the tolerance buckets on a real document.

What is the next real piece of work?

Nothing on this list. Expanding the rate corpus was the answer here until the corpus was removed, and the reasoning that removed it applies to anything of that shape: do not add a capability whose correctness depends on data somebody has to remember to refresh. What is left is watching the first live reports, which needs credits rather than engineering — and, when there are credits, running the two blocked verifications this document keeps labelling Unable to verify.

What changed about how this should be tested?

Five defects in one day were invisible to unit tests and visible the moment something rendered the output a customer receives. The instruments that found them — a document with known answers, a harness pointed at the real engine, the letters actually built — are cheap, run in one command, and needed no credits. Assert on what the customer receives, not on what the function returns.

The one-sentence version

Eighteen findings closed and five more caught on the way through; Closing is finished work at 86, HOA made the largest move to 80, and the only thing still genuinely unknown about either is what a real document does to them.

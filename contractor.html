What this does not do

Read this before you upload. It is the part most of this page is for.

  • We do not tell you a contractor is overcharging. Nobody publishes what your job should cost in your town this month. What we have is a set of published national installed-cost ranges, and the strongest thing they support is “this falls outside every one of them” — which is a question worth asking, not a verdict. The only real comparison is a second bid on the same scope, which is why the report pushes you towards one.
  • We do not check your contractor’s licence for you. We read the number off the estimate and send you to your state’s board to look it up, because a licence status is only worth anything read today, and looking it up yourself also shows you the bond, the insurance and any complaint history.
  • We do not contact your contractor. We draft the email. You read it, change it, and send it under your own name.
  • Not every check runs on every estimate. A lump-sum quote with no line items switches off the arithmetic checks. Your report names each one that could not run and what was missing, and if fewer than 60% ran we refund you.
  • Automated analysis, not legal or construction advice. Every finding shows its arithmetic or names its rule, so you can check it yourself and take it to your contractor, your building department, or an attorney.

Questions

What if I only have one quote?
Twenty-four of the twenty-six general checks still run, plus the trade-specific ones. The two you lose are the comparison checks, and your report says so by name. If you can get a second quote before you sign, it is the single most useful thing you can do — more useful than this report.
Why do you need my state?
Five states cap a home improvement deposit by statute and New York regulates where the money is held. The federal minimum efficiency for a split-system air conditioner is different in the north than in the south. And the licensing board you would look a contractor up at is different everywhere. None of that is on your estimate.
Do you tell me whether the price is fair?
Not in those words, because we would be guessing. We divide your price by the thing it is priced per — tons of cooling, roofing squares, a window count — and say where that falls against published national ranges, with the source named and the caveats stated. Then we compare your quotes against each other, which is the comparison that actually means something.
What if my estimate is just one number with no breakdown?
That is itself a finding, and one worth having: a lump sum cannot be compared, negotiated or disputed line by line. The arithmetic checks will be skipped and named as skipped. If enough of them are skipped that we fall under 60% coverage, you are refunded automatically.
My job is only a couple of thousand dollars. Is this worth it?
Probably not, and we would rather say so here than take the $49. The findings scale with the job: a deposit over a statutory cap on a $900 water heater is worth a few hundred dollars at most, and a missing completion date matters less when the work takes a morning. Under about $2,000, get a second quote instead. That costs nothing and it is better evidence than a national cost range. Come back when the number has five figures in it.
Will this work for a trade you do not list?
Pick “something else”. The twenty-six general checks run on any home improvement estimate — arithmetic, deposit, contract terms, sales practice. You will not get a trade-specific set, and the report will not pretend you did.
I have already signed. Is it too late?
Not necessarily, and the report says which findings survive a signature. A deposit over a statutory cap is still over the cap. If the sale was made at your home and you were never given the three-day cancellation notice, the clock generally has not started. Change orders and warranty terms are the ones that get much harder after signing, which is why they are marked that way.
How do I know you are not just an AI opinion?
Because the findings are not written by one. Reading your documents and judging them are two separate passes: the first copies the figures, the second runs a fixed list of checks over them in code. Every finding shows you the arithmetic or names the statute. Where a finding rests on a published range rather than a rule, it says so in those words. The only part written by a model is the opening summary, and it is given the findings, not your documents.
What if you find nothing?
Then you get that in writing, with every check named and the figure it was measured against. Most estimates are mostly fine, and knowing which twenty-nine things were checked before you hand over five figures is the thing you paid for. If fewer than 60% of the checks could run, that is a different result and you are refunded for it automatically.

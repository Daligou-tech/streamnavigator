How it works

You see what we found before you decide whether to buy the report.

  1. Name your bills and what they cost

    Internet, phone, insurance, utilities, gym, warehouse club. Most households have four to eight worth checking; fewer than three and there is usually not enough to compare.

  2. Answer the questions that apply

    Do you rent your modem. Are you on a promotional rate. Is the phone paid off. About twenty seconds a bill, and those answers are what the checks run on — a statement can show us the amount, but only you can tell us what you still want.

Seven checks

Every one of these is arithmetic on your own bill. We do not hold a database of what things cost and we will never tell you a bill is above market — nobody publishes anything trustworthy about what your neighbours pay, and a made-up comparison is worse than no comparison. What we can tell you is which lines on your own statement are yours to stop.

H1Equipment you rent that you could ownA gateway, modem, router or monitoring box billed monthly, forever. We price the rental against what buying one costs once.
H2A promotional rate with an end dateNot a saving — the opposite. We date it and tell you when to call, because this is usually the largest number in the house.
H3An instalment that outlived its deviceThe phone is paid off and the line is still on the bill. Also worth asking for the months since to be credited back.
H4Add-ons you do not useWire maintenance, channel packs, device protection, roadside. You name them; we price them.
H5Cover you are paying for twiceRoadside or rental-car cover on a policy when a card already carries it. We check you are not relying on it before we say a word.
H6An autopay or paperless discount not appliedMany providers offer one and apply it only when asked. We do not know whether yours does, so there is no figure here — only the question.
H7What this bill was a year agoYour own statement against your own statement. A rise you can name is much harder for them to defend than one you cannot.

Three things we will not do

  • We will not tell you a bill is above market. We hold no price table, we do not look prices up, and we are not going to invent a regional rate to make a report look thorough. Every figure here is a line on your own statement.
  • We will not tell you to drop cover you rely on. Duplicate cover is sometimes deliberate and two policies rarely exclude the same things. If you tell us you rely on it, that finding comes back as a question, not an instruction.
  • We will not put a figure on a line you did not price. No amount, no number. The report names those lines and asks you for the amount rather than estimating one and folding it into a total.

    What this does not do

    Read this before you start.

    • We do not connect to your bank. There is no login, no account access and no read-only link. You tell us what you pay for and you show us the bills.
    • We do not hold a price database and do not look prices up. So we cannot tell you whether your rate is competitive, and we will not pretend to. What we can do is read your own statement line by line, which is where the money on a household bill actually is.
    • We do not cancel or renegotiate anything for you. You get the finding, the figure and the script. You make every call yourself.
    • We do not know what a provider will offer you. A retention rate depends on who answers the phone. Where that matters we give you the ask and say plainly that we cannot promise the outcome.
    • We do not estimate a figure you did not give us. No amount, no number — the report names those lines and asks.
    • Some decisions are not ours. Cover you rely on comes back as a question with the reason named. That is the product working, not a gap in it.
    • We cannot make the work go away. Acting on a report with four findings takes about an hour and two of them will want a phone call. The report puts them in order and starts you on the largest, but you do the hour.

    Questions

    Do I need to connect my bank account?
    No, and there is no option to. You name your bills and upload them as PDFs or photos. No bank login is ever required, and the free scorecard is computed in your browser without sending anything at all.
    How do you know if I am overpaying?
    We do not, and we will not claim to. Nobody publishes a trustworthy table of what internet costs on your street this month, so a report that told you your rate was "above typical" would be guessing with a confident face. What we do instead is read your own bill for the things that are unambiguously yours to stop: equipment you rent, a promotion with an end date, an instalment for a phone you have paid off, add-ons you named, cover you hold twice. Those need no market data, and on most bills they are where the money is.
    What counts as a recurring bill?
    Anything billed to you on paper or by email: internet, TV, mobile, insurance, electricity and gas, water, home security or monitoring, gym and warehouse memberships. App and streaming subscriptions are a different problem — deciding those needs to know when you last used each one and whether you would miss it, which no statement can tell us. Subscription Navigator asks you directly, and costs $29.
    Why do you ask questions when I am sending you the bill?
    Because the bill shows the amount and not the answer. A statement cannot tell us whether you use the wire maintenance plan, whether the handset is paid off, or whether the roadside cover on your policy duplicates the one on your card. Those are the facts that turn a charge into a finding, and you are the only person who has them. The bills then let us read the lines you did not know to look for.
    Will you cancel things for me?
    No. You get the finding, the amount and the words to say. You make every change yourself.
    What if you find nothing?
    You will know that before you pay — the scorecard shows it free. What the report gives you then is every bill named, every check that ran on it, and the amounts behind them. If the report misses what it promised, we refund in full.
    What if my bills change later?
    Each audit is its own $49 report. There is no recurring charge, nothing to cancel, and equally nothing already paid for sitting unused. If your bills move enough to be worth re-checking, that is a fresh upload and a fresh report.
